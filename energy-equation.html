How to read the figure

The amber EGL (energy grade line) is the total head z + p/γ + V²/2g. It slopes gently downward because of friction (hL), jumps up by hp at the pump (energy added), and drops by ht at the turbine (energy extracted as shaft work).

The blue dashed HGL (hydraulic grade line) is the piezometric head z + p/γ — the level water would reach in a piezometer tube tapped into the pipe. It runs exactly V²/2g below the EGL. In the still reservoirs the velocity is ~0, so the two lines merge at the water surfaces; the HGL drops abruptly where the flow accelerates into the pipe and recovers where it decelerates back into reservoir 2.

The vertical stack halfway along the pipe dissects the total head at that section into its three parts: z is the height of the pipe above the datum, p/γ is the gap from the pipe up to the HGL, and V²/2g is the gap from the HGL up to the EGL. Try raising the velocity head: p/γ shrinks while the EGL stays put — kinetic energy trades off against pressure, which is Bernoulli’s principle sitting inside the energy equation.
